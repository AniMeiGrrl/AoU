### Third Canticle of the Caretaker

> Among the First Circle of the Divine stands [[Astrid - Veiled Goddess|Astrid]], whom the faithful call the Caretaker. Gentle is her hand, steadfast her heart, and perfect her charge. She keeps watch over the Sacred Veil, the barrier between life and death and between light and darkness, through which every soul must pass, from the least sparrow to the greatest emperor. Mistake her not for the Lady of Death. Death belongs to the turning of the ages, to the blade, to the storm, and to the will of Fate. Astrid claims no life before its appointed hour, nor does she hasten those whose thread has yet to be cut. Her work begins where all others end.
>
> When the final breath is drawn, the soul awakens before the Sacred Veil. There the Caretaker greets each traveler without judgment or fear. She gathers the weary from the burdens of flesh, comforts those whose hearts still cling to the living, and guides every spirit across the Veil along the path prepared since the First Dawn. No soul lingers. No soul is forgotten. No soul is misplaced. For nothing entrusted to the Caretaker is ever lost.
>
> The priests teach that every star upon the night sky is a lamp kindled along the Hidden Road. Though mortal eyes see only distant lights, the departed behold a path of endless radiance, where Astrid walks before them with her veil of silver and her Lantern of Dawn. Those who lived with kindness meet her as an old friend. Those who lived in cruelty meet her with the same gentle gaze. For mercy is not earned. Mercy is her nature.
>
> The Keepers of the Veil therefore serve every village alike. They bless the newborn, comfort the dying, tend the graves of strangers, and speak the Rites of Passing, that grief shall not harden into despair. They are forbidden from bearing weapons except in defense of another, for the hand that guides the departed must never hunger for blood. Upon every altar burns an unbroken flame whose light reflects the Lantern of Astrid herself. So long as the sacred fire is tended, the Great Passage endures without fail.
>
> Know this above all: there exists no darkness beyond the sight of the Caretaker. No shadow conceals the departed from her gaze. No distance bars her from those who call in their final hour. No prison forged by mortal hand, nor spirit born of malice, holds dominion beyond her reach. For the Sacred Veil is whole. As a mother finds her child in darkness, so does Astrid gather every soul entrusted to her keeping. Such is the order ordained since the First Dawn, and no force within Creation possesses the strength to sunder it.
>
> Therefore fear neither death nor darkness. Every road ends where the Caretaker waits, and every traveler, whether king, child, pilgrim, or pauper, shall find beneath her veil the same welcoming hand.

## Later History

The tome predates the banishment of the gods. In later generations, the [[Grand Ordinants]] of [[Othedias]] attempted to defile Astrid's name by calling her the **Taker of Souls**, teaching the faithful to fear the goddess once remembered for mercy. The oldest scripture contradicts that slander: Astrid never claimed lives or stole souls. She received only those whose mortal journeys had already ended and guided them across the Sacred Veil.

The Veil once marked an inviolate boundary between life and death, light and darkness. It now bears the vile stench of the Umbra. The missing souls are not trapped before the Veil; they are being taken and used for some unknown purpose before they can be entrusted to the Caretaker.

The party later found a spirit—or perhaps only a sacred light—bound within a ward deep underground. They freed it without knowing whether it was truly Astrid. When the light escaped, it pierced the darkness that had shrouded the world for more than a century. Through that opening, the party saw blue sky for the first time.

This was not proof that they had freed the Caretaker, but it was evidence that something divine had endured within the darkness.
